The case

What the complaint alleges

The lawsuit claims Intel made false and misleading statements during the class period, in violation of Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5.

01

Intel's exposure to the slowdown in PC end-market sales was greater than it told investors.

02

Channel partners were holding large amounts of unsold inventory, which limited Intel's ability to raise prices.

03

Gains from AI demand were being offset by weakness in the PC market.

04

When the market learned the truth, investors who bought during the class period allegedly suffered losses.

These are allegations only. No court has ruled on their merits, and the class has not been certified.

Lead plaintiff

What it means to lead the case

Under federal securities law, the court appoints one or more investors to represent the entire class. Courts generally look for the investor with the largest financial interest who can fairly represent other shareholders.

You do not have to serve as lead plaintiff to share in any recovery. If you do nothing, you may remain an absent class member.

Common questions

Does it cost anything to have my losses reviewed?

No. The initial case review is free and confidential, and submitting your information doesn't obligate you to anything.

I already sold my INTC shares. Can I still take part?

Possibly. What matters is when you bought. If you purchased during the class period and lost money, the firm can review your situation whether you still hold the shares or have sold them.

Do I have to be lead plaintiff to recover anything?

No. Serving as lead plaintiff is not required to take part in any recovery. Class members who take no action may remain absent class members.

What documents will I need?

Brokerage statements or trade confirmations showing your INTC purchases and sales. You can redact account numbers before sharing them.
